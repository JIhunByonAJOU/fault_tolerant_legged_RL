| Onset foot slip | 0.0826 m/s | 0.0720 m/s | -0.0106 m/s |

RMSE and other continuous entries are averages of per-robot-episode metrics,
not a single pooled time-step RMSE.  Recovery is an **any-occurring** 1-second
stable window within the post-failure horizon, not proof that stability was
maintained until the end of the episode.

Interpretation: JT71500 approximately preserves Teacher survival while using
no privileged failure information, and it reduces slip and action saturation.
It does **not** match Teacher velocity/yaw tracking or stable post-failure
recovery.  In particular, survival alone is an insufficient adaptation claim.
The marginal survival differences must not be described as a statistically
established superiority: robot episodes share seeds and terrain generation, and
Wilson intervals in the summary treat episode outcomes independently.

The strongest localized gap is the completely degraded `RL_hip_joint` at
random onset: Teacher/Student post-failure survival 90.97%/92.36%, but stable
recovery 60.42%/3.47%.  Student post-failure forward-speed RMSE is 0.1370 m/s
versus Teacher 0.0986 m/s; yaw-rate RMSE is 0.2877 versus 0.1867 rad/s.  This
points to continued movement without meeting the strict 1-second recovery
window (`|vx error|≤0.1 m/s`, `|yaw rate|≤0.2 rad/s`).  Per-step traces are
needed before attributing it to a specific latent-delay mechanism.

The joint-by-severity heatmap also exposes two large gaps at `d=0.8` that an
overall average hides: `RL_thigh_joint` recovery 96.53%/26.39% and
`RR_calf_joint` recovery 95.14%/36.81% (Teacher/Student), while both models
survive those cells at roughly 96--99%.  These cells should be included in the
same per-step diagnostic.  Visualization:
`logs/evaluations/p5-paired-tf43000-jt-students-v1/p5_joint_severity_heatmaps.png`.
