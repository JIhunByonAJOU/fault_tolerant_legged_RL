We propose a fault-tolerant locomotion policy based on reinforcement learning for quadruped robots under single-joint torque degradation. The policy maintains balance and tracks motion commands and reference paths under both nominal and degraded conditions. Using a Unitree A1 model in Isaac Gym, we train the policy through teacher–student joint training under conditions that include single-joint torque degradation and compare it with a nominally trained policy on identical mixed-terrain locomotion tasks. Experimental results show that the proposed policy achieves higher survival rates and better motion-command and path-tracking performance than the nominally trained policy, particularly under severe torque degradation. These results demonstrate that the proposed policy mitigates locomotion failures and tracking degradation caused by torque loss, enabling the robot to sustain commanded motion beyond merely maintaining balance.
