Quadrupedal robots should maintain balance and execute commanded motion after joint torque degradation. We present a teacher–student reinforcement learning control configuration whose history-based student handles nominal and single-joint degraded conditions without direct fault inputs. Unitree A1 simulations compare the fixed student with a nominally trained policy, each trained with one seed, on matched mixed-terrain courses using forward, diagonal, lateral, and sinusoidal-path tasks. At 100\% torque degradation, survival increased from 52.08\% to 89.24\%, while mean pre-termination cross-track root mean square error (RMSE) decreased from 0.225 to 0.080~m, averaged over the fixed task mixture. The results show simultaneous improvements in fall avoidance and motion tracking under severe torque degradation for these fixed policies and evaluated tasks.
