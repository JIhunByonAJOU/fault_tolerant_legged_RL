Quadrupedal robots should maintain balance and execute commanded motion after joint torque degradation. We present a teacher–student reinforcement learning control configuration whose history-based student handles nominal and single-joint degraded conditions without direct fault inputs. Unitree A1 simulations compare the fixed student with a nominally trained policy, each trained with one seed, on matched mixed-terrain courses using forward, diagonal, lateral, and sinusoidal-path tasks. At 100\% torque degradation, survival (non-fall rate) increased from 52.08\% to 89.24\%, while mean pre-termination $v_x$, $v_y$, and cross-track root mean square errors (RMSEs) decreased from 0.226 to 0.120~m/s, 0.262 to 0.112~m/s, and 0.225 to 0.080~m, respectively, averaged over the fixed task mixture. The results show simultaneous improvements in fall avoidance and motion tracking under severe torque degradation.
