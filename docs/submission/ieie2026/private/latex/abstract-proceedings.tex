We apply an existing teacher–student joint-training framework to quadruped locomotion under single-joint torque loss. Privileged teacher features are gradually replaced with history-based student features; evaluation uses only the student path without explicit fault information. Unitree A1 is evaluated in Isaac Gym across 12 joints and six loss levels. Under complete loss, survival increases from 41.49\% to 88.89\% compared with a policy trained only under normal conditions. Forward-velocity and yaw-rate root mean square errors (RMSE) decrease from 0.317 to 0.175~m/s and from 0.753 to 0.268~rad/s. Yet commands meet all three tolerances for only 41.07\% of the observation period, revealing tracking limitations despite improved survival.
